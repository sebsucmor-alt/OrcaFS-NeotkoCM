The Playground

The point of this fork was never a set of presets that work. It was a playground: mix, stack, experiment, and see what surface comes out. These are the same tools the feature pages use, with all the knobs left on.

Everything here runs the slicer's own maths in your browser. The colour compositing is ColorSci::blend_stacked, the dither is build_dithered_tools_*, and the colour distance is CIEDE2000. Nothing is sent anywhere, nothing needs the app open, and a recipe you land on here is a recipe you can type into the Sandwich Editor.

  • The Colour Mixer

    The big one. A filament library with real TD values, both Sandwich zones, every pass kind, and the printed colour resolving live. Compare two recipes side by side and read the ΔE between them.

    real optics real dither
  • Gamut & colour match

    Everything four filaments can reach by stacking, and an inverse ΔE2000 search: name a colour, get the closest recipe your spools can actually print, with an honest number for how close that is.

    ΔE2000
  • Pattern lab

    Every ColorStitch style with the real Bresenham underneath: blends, stripes, weaves, hand-typed strings. It reports how many tools a pass actually spends, which is the number that reaches the wipe tower.

    real algorithm
  • PathBlend staircase

    Drag the two ADV handles and watch the physical ramp rebuild itself, one print-height step per fill line, with the optical gradient it produces underneath.

    real geometry
  • TD explorer

    One filament over one base. The whole feature pack rests on this curve, and it is worth ten minutes of pulling the slider around before you tune anything else.

    Beer–Lambert
  • Layer height curve

    Draw a height curve over real layer bands, with control points and per-segment tension, and watch the bands redraw underneath it as you drag.

    bands from the curve

A few things to try

  • Load a clear filament into a slot and put it on top of everything. Translucent plastic is where most of the reachable colour lives, and a set of four opaque spools has a surprisingly small gamut.
  • Swap two passes in the stack and watch the colour move. The order is not decorative: light is absorbed on the way down and again on the way back up.
  • Shrink the surface until the dither has a dozen lines to work with. That is what happens on a small part, and it is the difference between a blend and a set of stripes.
  • Pick a colour you actually want in the matcher and see whether your filaments can get there. A ΔE above 5 is the honest answer that they cannot.
