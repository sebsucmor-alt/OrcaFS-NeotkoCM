ColorStitch

Two filaments, any ratio

Every fill line gets its own tool, in a sequence chosen so that any small window holds the ratio you asked for. The printer does not alternate to do it: it prints every line of one tool, then every line of the other.

What this is

Snapmaker Orca with a lot of extra features, built by one person who designs in 3D and reads code, rather than the other way round. Most of them started as "why can no slicer do this?". There is no roadmap; things get built when they get interesting. Every page here says how finished its feature actually is, and for a few of them the honest answer is never printed, look but don't trust.

Everything is opt-in. Leave the new settings alone and the build slices like stock Snapmaker Orca. Every release gets checked against that.

The demos are not mockups

The colour swatches, dither patterns and blend curves here are computed by JavaScript ports of the slicer's own functions: ColorSci::blend_stacked for the optics, build_dithered_tools_* for the patterns. Build a recipe on this site and you get the same number the app would give you. Where a demo is only a diagram, it says so on the demo.

New to all this? The seven-part video series is still the gentlest way in. It walks through one Sandwich from nothing to a printed part. Come back here afterwards for the reference.

Colour

The Sandwich, which is what most of this fork is about. Start at the top of the list.

  • §1 · §3 — start here The Sandwich

    Your top surface becomes a stack of 1–3 passes, each with its own tool, angle and share of the layer height. Build one here and watch the printed colour come out.

    no gate 2.3
  • §1 · §1e — in section The stack, in section

    The same Sandwich as a block you can turn round, cut open, with the composited colour on its top face. Then the question everyone gets to second: what the filament underneath does to every recipe you saved.

    no gate reference
  • §1b · §1d · §1f ColorStitch

    A pass that picks the filament for every single fill line: dithered blends, exact stripes, textile weaves, hand-typed patterns. The dither is Bresenham, so you get the same result every slice.

    no gate 2.3
  • §1c PathBlend

    A gradient made out of geometry. One filament climbs as a physical ramp inside the layer, one print-height step per fill line, and a second one caps the rest. The fade you see is optical.

    no gate fragile engine
  • §1e · §1g TD & the Studio

    Why a stack of passes reads as one colour. Transmission Density, Beer–Lambert, the reachable gamut from your four filaments, and an inverse ΔE2000 search for a colour you name.

    no gate
  • §6 The ColorStitch Painter

    Save Sandwiches as profiles and flood-fill them onto individual faces. Different steps of one object, each with its own recipe, and the weave drawn live on the model.

    no gate 2.4.0 rework

Seeing it

A striped G-code preview tells you very little about how a Sandwich will look. These pages close that gap.

  • §20 · §21 · §22 RealColor & Photo Mode

    no gate Libre for Prepare
  • §9 NeoTower

    A wipe tower planned after slicing, when every toolchange is known, including the sub-layer primes a Sandwich inserts inside a single layer. It is what makes adaptive height × multi-tool × Sandwich work at all.

    auto for Sandwich
  • §15 · §18 · §19 G-code & finishing

    A layer-ranged, per-tool G-code post-processor you edit as a chart. Real font kerning for embossed text. And bridges that anchor on solid ground before they cross.

Placing things

What happens when two objects touch, which stock slicers quietly get wrong.

  • §4 · §5 Libre Mode

    A two-key gate that unlocks the professional and experimental half of the fork: assemblies without a boolean union, per-volume compensation, full part settings, Simplify3D import, realistic shading.

    the gate itself
  • §7 · §17 Align & Stack · Gravity

    Place one object precisely against another, then make the slicer believe it: a face resting on another object prints as solid contact instead of a false bridge over thin air. Drag and drop that lands on the real surface below.

    True Objects
  • §24 Support Zones

    Point at the surface you want held up, then at where the column should land. The pillar is built between the two, its roof is the real patch with its curve on it, and the panel draws the section at true scale before you commit.

    Libre Mode engine print-verified
  • §12 · §16 Supports

    Support that routes around the other objects on the plate instead of growing through them, which nothing else in this slicer family does. Plus a wave-front roof that diffracts around concavities, over a hollow pillar.

    PerObject: no gate NeoWave: WIP

Structure & surface

Below the colour: how the walls are generated, how tall each layer is, what the surface feels like.

  • §8 · §13 · §14 Walls

    NeoArachne lets you pick which engine prints the outer wall, the inner walls and the gap fill, separately. Painter Pro Mode adds precision brushing and screen-space masks. NeoStitch interlocks layers without ever moving Z.

    Libre (NeoArachne) NeoStitch untested
  • §11 Precision layer height

    Draw layer height as an exact curve with control points and per-segment tension, over the object's real layer bands. Colour-aware guidance shades the heights your filaments can actually hold.

    Libre Mode
  • §23 Height Adaptive Effects

    The same curve pointed at any setting. The infill cell opens where nothing rests on it and closes under the top surface, at the same density and the same grams. Fuzzy skin is born, lives for a band, and dies again. No other slicer has it.

    Libre Mode structural or cosmetic
  • §10 Bump mapping & ZBump

    A PNG becomes physical relief: displacing wall centrelines around the object, per painted zone, or modulating the Z of the top-surface fill point by point into a real height field.

    Libre + env var expert only
